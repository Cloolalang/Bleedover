To normalize your $0\text{ dB}$ headroom walk test results down to an **LTE RSRP** (Reference Signal Received Power) coverage map for a **$20\text{ MHz}$ channel bandwidth**, we have to add one final layer of logarithmic math to the text file.

### The RSRP Normalization Math

As established, your CW tone concentrates 100% of its energy ($+20\text{ dBm}$) into a single frequency.

1. **The Multi-Carrier Share:** With $0\text{ dB}$ headroom, your single live carrier gets a maximum composite allocation of **$+12.2\text{ dBm}$** ($+20\text{ dBm} - 7.8\text{ dB}$ allocation for 6 carriers).
2. **The $20\text{ MHz}$ Bandwidth Subcarrier Splitting:** A standard $20\text{ MHz}$ LTE channel contains exactly **1,200 individual subcarriers** (Resource Blocks $\times$ 12 subcarriers per RB). RSRP measures the power of just *one* of these subcarriers.

To find out how much power lands on that single subcarrier, we calculate the bandwidth division factor:


$$\text{Bandwidth Factor} = 10 \log_{10}(1200) = \mathbf{30.8\text{ dB}}$$

### The Total RSRP Deduction

To find your total walk test plotting offset from your raw $+20\text{ dBm}$ CW tone down to a $20\text{ MHz}$ 1-CC RSRP value, you combine both factors:

$$\text{Total RSRP Offset} = \text{Multi-Carrier Share (-7.8 dB)} + \text{Bandwidth Splitting (-30.8 dB)} = \mathbf{-38.6\text{ dB}}$$

For ease of calculation in your walk test software, engineers round this to a **$-39\text{ dB}$ offset**.
