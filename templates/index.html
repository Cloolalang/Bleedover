    Method, assumptions and limitations

    Calculation. Expected downlink uses an estimated invoked system MCS mapped from DL RSRP (no CQI). RSRP is mapped linearly to MCS (0–28); throughput per carrier is derived from MCS and bandwidth using 3GPP TS 36.213 TBS (Tables 7.1.7.1-1, 7.1.7.2.1-1). Effective throughput = TBS-based layer throughput × MIMO factor; results are summed over all carriers. A protocol overhead (default 10%) is applied to the congestion-adjusted MAC-layer speed to give the estimated user/speed-test download (TCP/IP, RLC/PDCP overhead). Where the 36.213 table is unavailable, a legacy η_raw(RSRP) curve is used.

    Assumptions. Model assumes a Cat 6+ (4G+) / NR-capable UE. We distinguish MIMO (2×2) vs SISO (1×1): when the link is MIMO, your input is the fraction of time we assume 2 streams (vs 1 stream); the MIMO factor uses that and a 2× throughput gain for 2 streams vs 1. MCS is not measured (CQI not available); it is estimated from RSRP for indicative throughput only. RSRQ: RSRQ is highly variable (±4 dB in practice). In field experience, RSRQ is not seen better than about -7 dB; typical macro is around -13 dB. The optional RSRQ de-rate curve is indicative only (no external standard): impacts are assumed to start from -15 dB downwards; -20 dB to -24 dB causes larger speed reduction; -24 dB is effectively the bottom of the range and can reduce DL to ~1 Mbit/s on a 20 MHz channel.

    Functional method (optional). An indirect throughput estimate for existing coverage only (not used in the DAS estimator). If a functional method threshold (dBm) is set, carriers with average RSRP ≥ that threshold are assumed to provide peak/5 (peak = 150 Mbit/s for 20 MHz 2×2 MIMO → 30 Mbit/s per 20 MHz; divisor is fixed at 5, Swedish Post style). No congestion factor or RSRQ de-rate is applied. The threshold is compared to the displayed (raw) RSRP per carrier.

    Limitations. Outputs are indicative only and are not capacity or contractual guarantees. RSRP→MCS is an approximation (scheduler uses CQI/SINR in practice). Load, scheduler behaviour and backhaul are not modelled; congestion factors are indicative and not calibrated to this site—they reflect typical differences by environment, time of day and day of week. The RSRP calibration offset (dB) is for aligning the model with your real test data and is user-defined. Optional RSRQ (dB) per carrier de-rates that carrier’s estimated speed when quality is poor (indicative curve from -15 dB down to -24 dB; not from an external standard). RSRQ de-rating is based on empirical field-testing experience and is not bandwidth sensitive; further model tuning may be required. NR carriers use the same LTE-based mapping (use SS-RSRP). NSA NR (EN-DC) is implemented differently by different networks—aggregation and band combinations vary by operator. We do not distinguish MIMO type when rank > 1 is indicated (e.g. rank 2 vs rank 3/4); support for a rank distribution may be considered in a later version. MNO national benchmarks are from the Cross-report snapshot and should be refreshed when reports are updated. Reference: macro-pre-sales-radio-survey-requirements.md Section 7.2.
